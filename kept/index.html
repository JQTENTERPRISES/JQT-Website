Read the one star reviews and the complaint is never a missing feature. It is always one of four things.

  • It is slower than paper. So the crew quietly goes back to pen and a group chat, and the paper version wins, because paper is the one thing everybody can share.
  • It logs her out mid shift. She is holding a bucket on the third floor. She is not going to her inbox for a code.

The property Kept was built in already owned one of these. It was installed. It was paid for. It was dead. What actually ran the floor was a radio and a printed list, which is what runs most floors, in every brand, at every price point.

The one number Kept is judged on

On her second room, does she tap each step as she finishes it, standing in the room, or does she stand at the door at two in the afternoon and tap the whole day at once?

In flow taps mean the times are real and you own a record. Batch taps mean Kept became the old app with better colors. That is the whole test, it is the only one that matters, and you can check it yourself in an afternoon without asking us anything.

Which is why nobody gets trained. On day one the manager hands out a sticky note with a first name and four digits and says one sentence: your rooms are on your phone today. That is the entire rollout. If Kept needs a class, it has already lost, because the next property's crew will not get one either.

The record

Who did what. Proven.

Every system in this category will hand you a report. A report is a claim. Below is the difference between a report and a record, in three parts, each one something you can check without taking our word for it.

01

The gate

Mark clean does not press until every step of the room checklist has been tapped. Not greyed out with an override for the manager. It does not press.

A room that is clean because someone said so from a chair at 4pm is the thing your current data is made of. This is the mechanic that ends it.

02

The chain

Every action appends to a log that cannot be quietly edited. Not by staff, not by the manager, not by us.

Each entry is built from the one before it, so altering an old line breaks every line after it, visibly, on a page your manager can open while you stand there.

03

The anchor

Once a day the head of that chain goes to public timestamp authorities and the signed reply is kept.

The chain catches an edited row. The anchor catches the harder case: someone deletes the log and rebuilds a tidy fake whose hashes all recompute. To fake history you would also have to alter records held by a certificate authority. Verify one with openssl, without Kept, and without us.

The parts that break every other morning

Yesterday should not have to be re typed.

Most housekeeping tools are built around one clean day at a time. Real properties are not, and the gap between those two facts is where the manager's morning goes.

Out of order carries forward

With its reason attached. Nobody re marks the same four rooms every single morning, and no room quietly slips back into inventory because today's report did not know it was out.

Projects hold rooms across days

Recarpet 214. Repaint floor two. Ordered steps, a real deadline, many rooms at once, and a hold that survives every morning import until the work actually passes.

Modeled on how a PMS already treats an out of order room: a date range with a declared return status, not a checkbox somebody has to remember to tick again.

Supplies without a scanner

She taps LOW or OUT. That is her entire relationship with inventory, because a slower workflow is a dead workflow and barcodes on a housekeeping cart are how you get neither the count nor the clean.

The buy list assembles itself, and a floor alert overrides the ledger, because the shelf is more honest than the spreadsheet.

A safety button that knows the room

It already knows where she is, because she started the room. Locating the person is the expensive half of every system in this category, and here it is a column that has been filling itself in all day.

The alert is never queued offline. If it cannot reach the server she is told plainly, in her own language, to use the radio, because a queued panic alert is a lie told to someone in trouble. Raised, seen, and stood down each land on the chain, because nobody came and nobody was told are different facts.

Said plainly, because this is a legal area

Florida has no statewide employee safety device mandate. The brand this property flies is an AHLA 5 Star Promise signatory, so a staff safety device here is a brand standard expectation, not a legal one. Several mandates elsewhere call for dedicated Bluetooth hardware, which a phone is not. So Kept does not describe this as ESD compliance, in the product or on this page, and it will not start. If you are in a city with a real ordinance, we will tell you what Kept does and does not satisfy before you buy it.

The honest part

Three things Kept does not do.

You will find these out anyway. Better on a web page you are reading for free than on the third call.

01

It does not replace your PMS

Kept reads the morning room status report you already run, and at the end of the shift produces a sheet that mirrors your PMS columns so your lead re keys the clean rooms in one pass.

It never writes into your PMS. That is a decision, not a gap: writing into a PMS means a certification cycle, and you would be waiting on that instead of running your floor.

02

It holds no guest data

Room numbers, staff first names, checklists, and photographs of broken things. No guest names, no reservations, no cards.

There is nothing in it worth stealing, which is the correct amount of guest data for a housekeeping tool to be carrying around on twelve personal phones.

03

It runs one property

Multi property is designed and it is not built. If you own eight hotels, the honest answer today is that Kept would run one of them properly while we build the rest of the answer in front of you.

Some owners want exactly that. Most do not. Assume this is the state of it until we tell you otherwise in writing.

Questions owners ask first

The things you would ask on the call anyway.

Does Kept replace our PMS?

No. It reads the morning room status report you already run and produces an end of shift sheet that mirrors your PMS columns for your lead to re key in one pass. It never writes into your PMS.

Do we have to train the staff?

No, and we would rather you did not. Day one is a sticky note with a first name and four digits, and one sentence from the manager: your rooms are on your phone today. If Kept needs a class, it has already failed the test it exists to pass.

Our housekeepers do not all read English.

The floor app runs in English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole, and the room checklist is icon led, so the tap does not depend on the word. That was a requirement on day one, not a translation project bolted on later.

What if the morning report is late, missing, or malformed?

The day opens anyway, with every room dirty. A room wrongly listed dirty costs a walk through. A room wrongly listed clean costs a guest walking into an unmade bed. Kept picks the cheaper mistake on purpose, and the manager reconciles from the board rather than from a phone call.

Does it work in the stairwells and the back corners?

Her taps are held on the phone and sent when the signal comes back, and the screen says so plainly rather than pretending. The single exception is the safety alert, which is never queued: if it cannot reach the server she is told to use the radio instead.

Can staff or managers edit the log afterwards?

No, and neither can we. The log is append only and hash chained, so an altered entry breaks every entry after it. Once a day its head is anchored with public timestamp authorities, so a rebuilt history is visible rather than plausible.

What does it cost?

$499 to $799 per property per month, by size of property and how much of the operation you put on it. We put a number on what the confusion is costing you first. If we cannot find one bigger than the fee, we will tell you so.

Does Kept run a portfolio?

Not today. It runs one property properly. Multi property is designed and unbuilt, and you should assume that is the state of it until we say otherwise in writing.

What happens if our manager leaves?

The record does not leave with her. That is most of the point. Access is granted per person and revoked per person, a new manager is opened up from the back office rather than from a support queue, and the history she inherits is the same history that was there the day before.
